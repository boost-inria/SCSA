Semi-Classical Signal Analysis

A signal, read as the spectrum of a Schrödinger operator.

SCSA treats a positive signal as a potential well, solves the quantum eigenvalue problem that well defines, and rebuilds the signal from the squared eigenfunctions that fall out. Sharp, localised features need very few components — the components are pulse-shaped themselves, and they come from the signal rather than from a fixed dictionary.

yh(x) = 4h ∑n=1Nh κn ψn(x)² one parameter · h

How it works

Three steps, no dictionary

Fourier brings sine waves to the signal. Wavelets bring scaled bumps. SCSA brings nothing — it asks the signal which shapes it already contains.

STEP 1

Turn the signal upside down

Read the signal as a landscape and flip it: where the signal is large, the well is deep. That well becomes the potential in a Schrödinger operator, scaled by h.

STEP 2

Find what the well can hold

A well of finite depth holds finitely many bound states, each an eigenfunction ψn at an energy λn below zero. Deeper well, more states.

STEP 3

Add the squares back up

Square each eigenfunction, weight it by κn = √(−λn), and sum. The result reconstructs the signal, and each term is a localised piece of it.

For the curious and for the specialist

Without the mathematics

A sharp arterial pulse takes dozens of sine waves to describe, and three or four SCSA components.

That difference is the argument. Sine waves are spread out and a pulse is not, so describing one with the other is wasteful. SCSA's components are pulse-shaped because they were derived from a pulse-shaped signal.

Because they come from the signal itself, they tend to carry meaning. In arterial blood pressure the first component tracks the systolic peak and later ones pick up waves reflected from the periphery — quantities a clinician can name.

One number, h, sets the level of detail, and choosing it is choosing what counts as signal rather than noise.

With the mathematics

For a positive signal y ∈ L¹(ℝ) and semi-classical parameter h > 0, consider on L²(ℝ)

Hh(y) = −h² d²/dx² − y(x)

with negative eigenvalues λn = −κn² and L²-normalised eigenfunctions ψn. For a Riesz order γ > 0 and threshold λ, the reconstruction is

yh(x) = −λ + [ (h / Lclγ) ∑n (λ − λn)γ ψn(x)² ]2/(1+2γ)

with the classical constant Lclγ = Γ(γ+1) / (2√π Γ(γ+3/2)). In one dimension the standard choice is γ = 1/2 and λ = 0, where Lcl1/2 = 1/4 and the exponent becomes 1, so everything collapses to the form in the header:

yh(x) = 4h ∑n=1Nh κn ψn(x)²

The demos above use γ = 1/2 for the one-dimensional signals and γ = 2 for the images, following pyscsa's defaults. Either way yh → y as h → 0, and the number of components obeys the Weyl law Nh ∼ (1/πh)∫√y dx — which is what the sliders are really moving.

The weights are not a modelling choice. By Hellmann–Feynman, δλn/δy(x) = −ψn(x)². Applying this to the Riesz mean R3/2 = ∑ κn³ gives δR3/2/δy = (3/2) ∑ κn ψn², which is the γ = 1/2 reconstruction up to scale — and 3/2 is the unique Riesz order whose gradient returns weights linear in κn. The formula is that gradient: derived, not postulated. (The two orders are different objects — γ indexes the reconstruction, 3/2 indexes the mean being differentiated.)

  • hsemi-classical parameter, sets resolution
  • κn√(−λn), n-th spectral weight
  • ψnn-th bound state, normalised in L²
  • Nhnumber of negative eigenvalues
Where it is used

Applications

Origin

Arterial blood pressure

Introduced to decompose pressure waveforms into soliton-like components, separating the incident wave from its reflections and yielding physiologically meaningful indices.

Neural signals

EEG and spectroscopy

Sparse representation of transient, non-stationary recordings, where sharp bursts are what fixed dictionaries represent badly and adaptive components represent well.

Restoration

Denoising

Choosing h — automatically, with C-SCSA — suppresses noise while preserving peak amplitude and position, the features a low-pass filter tends to flatten.

Two dimensions

Image representation

The 2-D operator gives a compact spectral representation for compression, denoising and feature extraction — applied to brain MRI contrast enhancement and to carotid plaque classification from CT.

Outside biomedicine

Turbomachinery

Pressure signals in centrifugal compressors carry precursors of surge. The spectral decomposition picks them out early enough to be useful as a warning.
